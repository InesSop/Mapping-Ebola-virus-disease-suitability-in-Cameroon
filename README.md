# Mapping Ebola virus disease spillover suitability in Cameroon

## Overview

This repository contains the R scripts used to develop spatial suitability maps for Ebola virus disease (EVD) spillover in Cameroon using a weighted linear combination multi-criteria decision analysis (WLC-MCDA) framework.

The modelling approach integrates environmental, climatic, ecological, wildlife-related and anthropogenic factors potentially associated with Ebola virus spillover. Two complementary sources of knowledge were considered:

1. **Scientific literature**, using risk factors for which published studies reported evidence of an association with Ebola virus occurrence or spillover; and
2. **Field-expert knowledge**, using risk factors and relative weights derived from expert elicitation.

Separate models were developed for the **dry** and **wet** seasons. Seasonal models were subsequently integrated to derive annual suitability surfaces, and the literature-based and expert-based annual models were combined with equal contributions to obtain the final EVD spillover suitability surface.

The repository also contains scripts for model sensitivity analysis and for an exploratory spatial comparison with published human and wildlife serological observations.

---

## Scientific interpretation

The maps generated by this workflow represent **relative spatial suitability for potential Ebola virus spillover**.

They should not be interpreted as:

- probabilities of Ebola virus occurrence;
- predictions of future EVD cases or outbreaks;
- estimates of infection prevalence; or
- maps of confirmed Ebola virus circulation.

Cameroon has not reported documented human EVD outbreaks or confirmed clinical cases. Consequently, direct external validation against observed human EVD occurrence was not possible.

The final maps are therefore interpreted as spatial estimates of **relative spillover suitability**.

---

## Modelling framework

The analysis follows a weighted linear combination approach:

\[
S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i x_i
\]

where:

- \(S\) is the modelled spillover suitability index;
- \(x_i\) is the standardised spatial layer for risk factor \(i\);
- \(w_i\) is the corresponding factor weight; and
- the weights sum to 1.

All risk-factor layers used by the WLC models were standardised prior to model implementation.

## Scripts

### `01_wlc_literature.R`

Constructs the literature-based EVD spillover suitability models for the dry and wet seasons.

Risk factors and their relative weights were derived from the scientific literature reporting evidence of associations between candidate factors and Ebola virus occurrence or spillover.

The script:

- loads the standardised risk-factor rasters;
- aligns the raster layers to a common spatial grid;
- verifies raster geometry;
- applies the literature-derived weights;
- calculates the WLC suitability index; and
- exports separate dry- and wet-season suitability surfaces.

---

### `02_wlc_experts.R`

Constructs the expert-based EVD spillover suitability models for the dry and wet seasons.

Risk-factor selection and relative weighting were derived from field-expert elicitation. Separate weighting schemes are used for the dry and wet seasons to account for seasonal differences in the perceived contribution of individual spillover risk factors.

The script follows the same spatial WLC framework as the literature-based model.

---

### `03_annual_mean_models.R`

Generates annual source-specific suitability surfaces.

For each knowledge source, the dry- and wet-season suitability surfaces are averaged.

This produces:

- an annual literature-based EVD suitability surface; and
- an annual expert-based EVD suitability surface.

---

### `04_combined_annual_model.R`

Integrates the annual literature-based and expert-based suitability surfaces.

Equal importance is assigned to the two knowledge sources.

The resulting raster represents the final annual EVD spillover suitability surface used for the principal spatial interpretation.

The script also generates a literature-minus-expert difference surface to describe spatial agreement and disagreement between the two knowledge-based modelling approaches.

Positive difference values indicate relatively greater suitability according to the literature-based model, whereas negative values indicate relatively greater suitability according to the expert-based model.

---

### `05_sensitivity_analysis.R`

Evaluates the robustness of the WLC model to uncertainty in risk-factor weights.

Each baseline factor weight is independently varied from **−25% to +25% in 5% increments**.

When the weight of a selected factor is modified, all remaining weights are proportionally rescaled so that the sum of the weights remains equal to 1.

For each perturbation, a new suitability surface is generated.

Two spatial measures of model sensitivity are subsequently calculated.

#### Pixel-wise standard deviation

The standard deviation of suitability values obtained across perturbed models is calculated for every raster cell.

Higher values indicate areas where modelled suitability is more sensitive to variation in the factor weights.

#### Average Relative Change

The Average Relative Change (ARC) describes the average magnitude of relative change between the baseline model and the perturbed models.

This provides a complementary measure of local sensitivity to uncertainty in the factor weights.

Sensitivity analyses are performed separately for the dry and wet seasons.

---

### `06_exploratory_serology_comparison.R`

Performs an exploratory spatial comparison between modelled EVD spillover suitability and published serological observations.

Because Cameroon has not reported documented human EVD outbreaks or confirmed clinical cases, the serological observations are not treated as an independent external validation dataset.

Instead, they are used to assess whether locations with published evidence of Ebola virus seroreactivity occur in areas assigned relatively elevated modelled spillover suitability.

#### Human serological evidence

Published human serological observations are used as the primary evidence of potential previous human exposure.

Two scenarios are considered:

- **Strict human scenario** — restricted to confirmed human seropositive sites;
- **Sensitive human scenario** — additionally includes sites showing unconfirmed human serological reactivity.

Human sampling locations retained for the comparison include **Djoum, Ebolowa and Sangmelima**.

The corresponding observations were based on serological evidence for Zaire ebolavirus (EBOV), including assays targeting viral antigens such as glycoprotein (GP), viral protein 40 (VP40) and nucleoprotein (NP).

#### Wildlife serological evidence

Serological observations from *Eidolon helvum* sampled in Yaoundé are included as complementary ecological evidence.

Because antibody cross-reactivity may occur and viral RNA was not detected, bat seroreactivity is not interpreted as evidence of confirmed local EBOV infection.

Human and wildlife evidence are therefore analysed separately.

#### Non-season-specific suitability surfaces

Because published serological sampling periods cannot reliably be assigned to a specific season, two composite surfaces are considered:

- **Mean seasonal suitability**, representing average suitability across the dry and wet seasons;
- **Maximum seasonal suitability**, representing the highest suitability assigned to each pixel in either season.

The maximum surface is included as a complementary representation of locations that may become comparatively suitable during at least one season.

#### Spatial extraction

For each serological sampling location, suitability is extracted:

1. from the raster cell containing the reported sampling location; and
2. as the mean suitability within a **10 km buffer** around the sampling location.

The buffer analysis accounts for spatial uncertainty in reported sampling locations and for potential local movements of humans and wildlife hosts.

#### Empirical comparison

Sampling-site suitability values are compared with the broader distribution of suitability across the mapped study area.

The script calculates:

- pixel-level suitability;
- mean suitability within a 10 km buffer;
- empirical suitability percentiles;
- descriptive summaries by evidence scenario; and
- exploratory randomisation-based comparisons.

Background locations represent the spatial distribution of available modelled suitability and must not be interpreted as confirmed EVD absences.

Because the number of available serological sampling sites is very small, all resulting comparisons are interpreted descriptively and cautiously.

## Methodological inspiration and relationship to previous work

The analytical framework and development of the R workflow were informed by the approach presented by **Lee-Cruz et al. (2021)**:

> Lee-Cruz L, Lenormand M, Cappelle J, Caron A, De Nys H, Peeters M, Bourgarel M, Roger F, Tran A. (2021). *Mapping of Ebola virus spillover: Suitability and seasonal variability at the landscape scale*. **PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases**, 15(8), e0009683. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0009683

Lee-Cruz et al. developed a GIS-based multi-criteria evaluation framework to map spatial and seasonal suitability for Ebola virus spillover by integrating environmental, climatic, anthropogenic and potential host-related factors. Their study also assessed model sensitivity and compared mapped suitability with documented spillover locations.

The authors made the R code associated with their study available as Supporting Information. This code provided an important methodological reference for the development and organisation of the present workflow.

The scripts in this repository are **not direct reproductions of the Lee-Cruz et al. code**. They were independently developed and adapted specifically for the Cameroonian context, the risk-factor datasets available for this study, the literature- and expert-based weighting strategies, the seasonal modelling design, the sensitivity analyses, and the exploratory comparison with published serological observations.

Users of this repository are encouraged to consult and cite the original Lee-Cruz et al. (2021) study when applying or adapting similar GIS-MCDA approaches to Ebola virus spillover suitability mapping.

## Input data

The workflow requires preprocessed and standardised spatial risk-factor layers.

Depending on the model, these may include variables representing:

- climatic conditions;
- forest and vegetation characteristics;
- deforestation;
- elevation;
- bushmeat hunting pressure;
- bushmeat trade;
- human population density;
- socioeconomic conditions;
- bat occurrence or suitability; and
- non-human primate occurrence or suitability.

All layers used in the WLC calculations should be spatially compatible and transformed onto a common relative suitability scale before model implementation.

The scripts in this repository focus on the modelling and analytical stages. Upstream procedures used to acquire, preprocess and standardise the original spatial datasets are not necessarily reproduced here.

Users wishing to reproduce the workflow with their own data should therefore prepare equivalent standardised spatial layers before running the WLC scripts.
